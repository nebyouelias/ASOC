Getting to Addis Ababa

Everything you need to plan your journey to ASOC 2026, flights into Bole International, visas, ride apps, money, connectivity, and what to expect in Africa’s diplomatic capital.

Arriving in Ethiopia

Flying into Addis Ababa

Addis Ababa is one of Africa's easiest capitals to reach. Bole International Airport (ADD) is the hub of Ethiopian Airlines, Africa's largest carrier, with direct connections to 60+ African cities and major hubs across Europe, North America, the Middle East, and Asia. The airport sits just 6–8 km from the city's main hotel districts.

✈️

Bole International Airport (ADD)

Modern international terminal, 20–40 minutes from most conference hotels depending on traffic. Ethiopian Airlines offers convenient one-stop connections from most of the world through its Addis hub.

  • Arrive at Terminal 2 for most international flights
  • Currency exchange, ATMs, and SIM counters in arrivals
  • Hotel shuttles: many hotels offer free airport pickup, request when booking
🛂

Visa & Entry

Most visitors can obtain an Ethiopian e-Visa online before travel, the smoothest option, via the official portal, or a visa on arrival at Bole (available to many nationalities).

  • Apply online at the official portal: evisa.gov.et
  • Passport valid 6+ months recommended
  • Yellow fever certificate required if arriving from a country with risk of transmission
  • Requirements change, verify with your nearest Ethiopian embassy before booking

Moving Around

Getting around Addis Ababa

Addis is a sprawling, lively city. For visitors, ride-hailing apps are by far the easiest and safest way to move around, cars are metered through the app, so there's no fare negotiation.

📱

Ride-Hailing Apps

  • RIDE, the most established local app; can also be ordered by phone (8294)
  • Feres, popular, widely available, card/cash options
  • ZayRide, another reliable local operator

Download before you arrive; a local SIM makes pickup coordination much easier.

🚕

Taxis & Shuttles

  • Blue-and-white street taxis are plentiful, agree the fare before departing
  • Yellow airport taxis operate from Bole with posted counters
  • Hotel cars can be arranged at reception for fixed rates
🚈

Public Transport

  • The Addis Ababa Light Rail crosses the city on two lines, though coverage is limited for visitors
  • Minibuses are the city's workhorse but are best left to locals unless you're adventurous
  • For conference days, ride apps and hotel shuttles reach the Adwa Memorial Museum directly

Practical Essentials

What travelers should know

💵

Money

  • Currency: Ethiopian Birr (ETB)
  • Exchange money at banks or the airport, keep receipts
  • Major hotels accept cards; carry cash for taxis, cafés, and markets
  • ATMs are common at banks and hotels in Bole and Kazanchis
  • Telebirr mobile money is used everywhere by locals
📶

SIM Cards & Internet

  • Ethio Telecom and Safaricom Ethiopia sell tourist SIMs at the airport, bring your passport
  • Data packages are inexpensive; 4G covers the city
  • Hotel Wi-Fi is standard at conference-grade properties
⛰️

Altitude & Health

  • Addis sits at ~2,355 m (7,726 ft), one of the world's highest capitals
  • Take it easy the first day: hydrate, go light on alcohol and caffeine
  • Malaria risk in the city is low due to altitude; consult your travel clinic for personal advice
  • Drink bottled or filtered water
🌤️

November Weather

  • Dry season, expect sunny days around 20–23°C (68–73°F)
  • Evenings are cool, often 8–10°C (46–50°F), bring layers and a jacket
  • Time zone: East Africa Time (UTC+3), no daylight saving
  • Language: Amharic; English is widely used in business and hotels
Coming for ASOC 2026?

Sessions are held at the Adwa Memorial Museum (Adwa Victory Memorial) in Piassa, central Addis Ababa — see it on the map. November is conference season in Addis: book flights and hotels early, allow generous time for traffic on session mornings, and if your driver does not recognize the English name, show them የአድዋ ድል መታሰቢያ.
